Guide Me, O Thou Great Jehovah

"Guide Me, O Great Jehovah" is one of the oldest and most loved pilgrim hymns in Christianity.

The Author
William Williams Pantycelyn. Written by Wiiliam Williams(1717-1791),

known as "The Sweet Singer of Wales." He was originally a deacon in the Church of England who became a Calvinistic Methodist preacher. He travelled on horseback all over Wales preaching, and wrote over 800 hymns.

The Original Language
He wrote it in Welsh, not English. The Welsh title was Arglwydd, arwain trywy'r anialwch which means "Lord, lead me through the wilderness."

It was first publilshed in 1745 in his collection Alleluia with the title "A Prayer for strength to go through the wilderness of this world." It had 5 verses. It was published again in 1762.

The English Translation
In 1771, another Welshman, Peter Wiiliams(no relation), translated verse 1,3 and 5 into English for his book Hymns on Various Subjects. That translation began "Guide me, O Thou great Jehovah."


Later that same year, William Williams himself translated it for the Countess of Huntingdon's Chapel Hymnal. The English version we sing today is a combination of both translations.
That's why you see two versions today:
    • "Guide me, O thou great Jehovah"- the original translation.
      "Guide me, O thou great Redeemer"- a later change in many hymnals to make it more Christ-centered.

      The Hymn

      Guide me, O Thou great Jehovah,
      Pilgrim through this barren land.
      I am weak, but Thou art mighty;
      Hold me with Thy powerful hand.
      Bread of heaven, bread of heaven,
      Feed me till I want no more; Feed me till I want no more
